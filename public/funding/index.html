Holding unlocks a bounded allowance

A qualifying wallet can use the published allowance while it remains eligible. Tokens stay in the wallet. More API keys do not add quota, unused allowance does not roll over, and a token purchase does not create a refundable service deposit.

A verified mint, threshold, and limits must be published first. An unconfigured mint or ticker is not an invitation to buy.

Provider funding is an operator responsibility

The operator acquires native GNK and funds the deposit address shown in its dedicated OpenBroker dashboard. A registration wallet, public reserve address, self-custody balance, and dashboard deposit address can be different; using the wrong one can lose funds.

Holdbyte checks spendable provider credit before dispatch and maintains a separate local allowance ledger. An admin allocation records funds already verified by the operator; it does not move GNK. Reservations are conservative estimates, and a final provider charge can be higher.

Transfer research is not customer instructions

Documented acquisition and WGNK bridge routes are dated operator research. Fees, custody, and availability can change. Customers should not send SOL, SPL tokens, WGNK, or other assets to any Gonka or OpenBroker address shown in product documentation.

The documented WGNK return route derives the native receiving account from the same signing key used for the Ethereum burn. It is not an arbitrary-recipient swap. The public server has no treasury signing key and cannot swap, bridge, launch, claim fees, buy back, burn, or distribute tokens.

Market activity is not operating cash

Collected creator fees or future earned service revenue may replenish compute after costs and obligations. Market capitalization, unclaimed fees, unsold team tokens, and user lock principal are not spendable revenue. Zero trading produces zero creator-fee income while service costs can continue.

Launch venues, burns, and distributions remain proposals in the dated token design research. They are not holder entitlements or automated customer actions. Re-check primary provider and bridge documentation before any operator transfer.

How optional buybacks and burns would work

Not live: Holdbyte does not collect trading fees automatically or execute token swaps, buybacks, or supply burns. A future project-owned treasury may receive finalized creator-fee proceeds. Its administrators, signer threshold, transfer powers, fee destinations, and transaction history must be published before users are asked to rely on it.

After provider costs, tax obligations, operating expenses, and reserve requirements are met, the operator could choose to purchase the project token with an approved amount of surplus. A verified BurnChecked transaction can then permanently reduce mint supply. A planned allocation or transfer to another address is not evidence that a burn occurred. Buyback frequency, percentage and continued operation cannot be promised before the mechanism and funding exist.

Pump.fun documents an optional Tokenized Agent buyback-and-burn mechanism for eligible launches. It is platform-controlled and distinct from Holdbyte's own vault or earnings. A creator-fee stream should not be counted as Holdbyte revenue until the funds are received and reconciled. See the platform disclosure and the project's proposed treasury design.

Why user deposits cannot be hidden inside the treasury

A proposed lock vault would hold qualifying user-owned tokens until a published unlock time, with owner withdrawal after maturity. Locking does not reduce total supply or create provider credit. Developer control over project-earned fees is different from authority to move, seize, or spend deposited customer tokens. Any operator custody or upgrade authority must be disclosed before a deposit, alongside its withdrawal and loss risks.

No staking contract has been deployed by this repository. Until one is audited and activated, users should not transfer tokens to any address claiming to provide Holdbyte staking.
