Holding the project token does not create a redemption claim on the GNK treasury. AI tokens are usage units, not the Solana coin.

Native GNK is the compute asset

OpenBroker bills in GNK-native accounting. Its current documentation states a live DevShard protocol rate of 10 ngonka per token per attempt, with retries/racing commonly increasing the effective rate. Slipvolt therefore treats the provider balance and its own allowance ledger as finite budgets.

Two acquisition paths to verify

Official documented path: Gonka’s FAQ currently describes obtaining WGNK on Ethereum and bridging it back to native GNK. Third-party route candidate: HOT currently advertises HOT Exchange / NEAR Intents swaps from SOL, USDC and other assets into native GNK. That is useful for treasury operations, but it is not an endorsement by Gonka or a guaranteed quote. Confirm the destination is native GNK in an owner-controlled Gonka wallet, inspect fees/minimum received, and test a small amount before using operating funds. HOT Gonka integration ↗

WGNK → native GNK

Gonka’s current FAQ warns that coins claiming to be GNK on Solana are not official GNK. The only official Ethereum WGNK/bridge contract documented by Gonka is 0x972a7a92d92796a98801a8818bcf91f1648f2f68. Returning WGNK burns it on Ethereum and releases native GNK to the Gonka address derived from the same key that signed the Ethereum burn.

The bridge is not a swap. If treasury funds begin as SOL, USDC, or another asset, the operator must first use a separately verified trade/cross-chain route to obtain WGNK or native GNK. Every route should be quote-checked and tested with a small amount before operating funds move.

Fund the assigned OpenBroker deposit address

After native GNK reaches an owner-controlled Gonka wallet, the operating amount is sent to the OpenBroker deposit address assigned in that broker account’s dashboard. OpenBroker explicitly warns not to send the deposit to the personal wallet entered at registration. The service verifies spendable provider balance separately from any self-custody reserve.

Creator fees can replenish compute—but are not guaranteed

A future Pump.fun launch can create creator-fee revenue when eligible trading occurs. Pump.fun currently documents a 0.300% creator fee on bonding-curve trades; canonical PumpSwap creator rates vary by market-cap tier after graduation. That revenue is separate from market capitalization and from the liquidity pool itself. Realized fees can be recorded as operating revenue, then allocated according to the published reserve and developer policy. No trading-volume or profitability assumption is built into customer access.

Launch-mode tradeoff: Pump.fun’s current holder rewards mode redirects the creator fee to holders, leaving nothing for the creator to claim. If creator fees are intended to replenish Slipvolt compute, the launch configuration must remain a regular creator-fee coin rather than a holder rewards coin. Holder AI access is provided by Slipvolt itself and does not require Pump.fun holder rewards. Current Pump fee schedule ↗

What the software will not do automatically

The public server holds no treasury signing key and cannot execute a swap, bridge, Pump.fun fee claim, or developer payout. Operator tooling is deliberately read-only or local-accounting focused until routes, destinations, custody, and legal/accounting policy are reviewed.
