Status of these rules

This is a draft of expected service behavior, not a final sales contract. No token purchase is executed by this app. When the operator publishes the official token link, it opens Pump.fun; any trade happens there. Paid service checkout is not enabled. The operator must publish final terms, its identity, and a private contact before accepting customers or funds.

What holder access provides

A qualifying wallet can create revocable API keys and use a bounded allowance while capacity and funding are available. It is not a treasury share, prepaid deposit, GNK claim, dividend, or redemption right. There is no guaranteed return, token price, resale liquidity, or uninterrupted access.

Published quotas, model limits, holdings, account status, maintenance, and available GNK govern requests. Allowance does not roll over or redeem for cash. Holding more than the threshold does not automatically multiply it.

No staking contract, token-payment settlement, or automatic treasury swap is implemented. A proposal is not a live customer benefit. Verify a token by its published mint, not its ticker.

Use the service responsibly

Use wallets and content you are authorized to use. Protect API keys. Do not bypass limits, attack the service, rotate wallets to drain shared funding, or expose private operator credentials. The operator can suspend new requests when needed to protect the service.

Review model output before relying on it. Your application must validate tool arguments and authorize actions; generated output does not authorize a transaction or payment.

Charges, failures, and refunds

Holder access has no second payment step. Rate-card fields are operator planning inputs; automatic checkout and overage billing are disabled. Buying a market token does not create service credit.

An interrupted or uncertain provider result can leave allowance reserved for review. Cancellation does not prove zero upstream cost. Revoking a key does not erase earlier usage or stop a request already dispatched.

No paid-service refund workflow is implemented. Before enabling payment collection, final terms must cover unused credit, billing errors, failed delivery, refunds, and support. This draft does not declare all sales final.

Funding and developer compensation

The operator allocates native GNK for inference. Funding may come from operator capital, collected creator fees, or future earned usage revenue. Buying tokens on a market does not deposit funds into Holdbyte’s compute account.

Developer compensation remains a proposal from distributable surplus after service costs, customer obligations, and reserves. Planning scenarios are not holder profit shares. Market capitalization, unclaimed creator fees, and user lock principal are not earned operating revenue.

Token rights, staking and treasury controls

Current implementation: token holdings are checked for access. No user-deposit vault, staking yield, buyback executor, burn executor, or automatic fee-to-GNK conversion is operating in this application. A token listing alone does not enable any of these functions.

Proposed funding: a separately identified project treasury may receive collected creator fees, pay operating expenses and provider credit, and optionally spend verified surplus on token repurchases and burns. Any creator fees depend on the final launch program, pool, trading volume and actual collection. No trading volume or burn is guaranteed.

Proposed locking: users would have to consent to published lock duration, custody/upgrade powers and withdrawal rules before depositing any tokens. The design currently recommends owner-controlled withdrawals after maturity and no developer sweep over user principal. A different custodial design would require clear, specific disclosure before deposit, not an assumption that “staking” grants operator withdrawal rights.

A lock removes tokens from circulation temporarily; only a finalized on-chain token burn reduces total supply. Neither lock nor burn directly pays the AI provider. Before launch, publish the mint, supply and mint/freeze authorities, treasury and vault addresses, upgrade authority, fee recipients, eligibility cutoff and benefit-change policy.

What must exist before launch

The operator still must publish the verified mint and buy route, eligibility and allowance, funding policy, team allocations and vesting, applicable fees, operator identity, private support, privacy and retention details, payment and refund terms, and a policy-change process.
